# 1.3.4-alpha.3

## Added

- Online play recovers from a simulation divergence instead of ending: every peer records the level's confirmed inputs, and when a guest's periodic digest disagrees with the host's, the host sends it that record and the guest reloads the level and replays it to where everyone else stands while the others wait, then play continues. Terminal disagreements still end the match.
- Online play shows the ping in the corner of the screen under a signal-strength mark of four rising bars: all four lit green while the round trip is under 60 ms (an input delay of at most one tick), three lit yellow under 120 ms, two lit orange under 200 ms, one lit red beyond, and none while the ping is unknown. The rollback's own figures (ticks replayed and corrected, stalls, the confirmed tick) go to the log every five seconds.
- When another player's inputs stop arriving, online play says so after a second with a countdown, and at fifteen seconds the host drops that player and the game goes on instead of freezing until the connection times out. A guest whose host has gone quiet still leaves at the deadline.
- A guest that disconnects, leaves or stops responding during online play is dropped rather than ending everyone's game: the host tells the others, the dropped player's characters stand still from the last tick its input reached the host, a shop purchase it had under way is abandoned, a player dropped for not responding is told so, and a notice on screen says the game goes on. Losing a player before the first stage begins still ends the match.
- Online compatibility is judged by what a build does rather than which binary it is: the identity now digests the sources and version the game was built from and a witness of its arithmetic (the x86-64 profile, rounding and exception modes, and the results of a fixed corpus of the math the simulation uses), and every build keeps floating-point operations as written instead of fusing them. A build of the same commit by another compiler or on another machine joins when its numbers agree.
- The simulation's sines, cosines, tangents, their inverses, exponentials, logarithms, powers, hypotenuses and blends are computed by the game's own portable routines instead of each platform's C library, which round a few of them differently; the results are within an ulp of correctly rounded and bit-identical on Windows and Linux, so a Windows build and a Linux build of the same commit can share an online room. Their witnesses are tested to agree; a full cross-platform session has not yet been played.
- In a room of three or four, the guests connect to each other directly as well as to the host, so one guest's inputs reach another without crossing the host first; each guest's address travels with the room's roster and the pairs link while the lobby is still open. The host still relays everything and decides everything, so a direct link that cannot be made or that drops costs nothing but the shortcut.
- Game Options has a Language setting: it lists every language with a text table under `data/text` by the name the table gives itself (`language.name`), and choosing one writes the menus in it at once and a level's own messages from the next level loaded. Only English ships so far, with the Latin glyphs lifted from the game; a language whose glyphs the disc's fonts lack will draw them from a font extension once one is packed.
- A second mouse-and-keyboard scheme, Robotron, chosen per player under Settings > Controls as the Control Scheme row beneath the input device: the keys walk the character the camera's way (W up the screen, S down, A and D across) facing the way it walks, and holding an attack or a throw plants it and turns it to where the mouse points, so it never strafes or slides. Tank, the scheme so far (facing the mouse; W toward it, S away from it, A and D sidestepping), stays the default. Online, the scheme travels with each player's inputs, so the host walks them the same way.
- The host picks the online startup input delay from the lobby's measured round trip: none under 20 ms, one tick under 60 ms, two beyond.
- The Auto Aim control option is back, under Game Options > Combat and as `combat.autoAim` (on by default): off, thrown weapons, the familiar's spit and a standing attack keep the way the player faces instead of turning toward the nearest enemy, boss, barrel or wall, as the original's per-pad Auto Aim option did. An online room agrees the setting as part of its rules.

## Fixed

- Online input prediction no longer misses almost every tick of a mouse-aimed player: sampled movement and aim are rounded to coarse steps so a steady hand repeats the same command, roughly halving corrections while a mouse-aimed character moves (four ticks in five became about one in three on the same machine).
- Shooting a bottle lying about breaks it again: a thrown weapon leaves the hand up to four units off the floor and flies level, and the bottle's authored two-unit height let every archer's arrow pass over it, so a bottle now counts along the whole column a throw passes through. Familiar and phoenix shots still fly through bottles.
- Settings > Controls draws its list of players at the size of the other option pages instead of in the small rows of a player's bindings, and the page is heard like the others: moving the cursor, choosing a player or an action, turning the device or page with an arrow, binding a control and stepping back each sound. Choosing a Back row anywhere in the settings now sounds the way the Back button does. Behind the scenes every settings page now makes its rows the same way, so a page fits and reads like the rest.
- Pickups lying about spin round without a pause: a figure whose spin is authored with its first frame held for a frame and repeated a whole turn on at its end (the Reflective Shield and the Hammer among them) stood still for a tenth of a second on every turn; a lying pickup now cycles only the frames that make one turn.
- The haze over the Trenches no longer cuts off the generators, players, enemies and pickups standing under it: a solid wearing a translucent texture is now drawn after the figures beneath it and blends over them, where before (and, by the original's draw order, on the console too) it was drawn first and rejected everything behind it. Binary cutouts such as fences and wheat keep their place among the solids.
- Costume pieces the class animation does not name no longer float at their rest place while the body moves: the blue knight's levelled plates and plume, the red valkyrie's fur and the yellow jester's shoulders now ride the limb they are attached to.
- Interrupting a push no longer leaves its lint running: on Windows the pre-push hook runs behind the wrappers of a Python shim and never saw the interrupt, so its lint and sixteen clang-tidy workers carried on for nobody. The hook now watches the git that ran it and ends the lint, workers and all, as soon as git is gone, and a lint that is interrupted or whose caller is gone ends its own workers.
